# Literature Review: Analytical Swing-Up Control of the Furuta Pendulum

The swing-up control task is to drive the pendulum from hanging down to the upright position using the motor at the base. Several works have derived **analytical, closed-form control laws** for this underactuated nonlinear system (and its cart-pole analog), using physics-based energy arguments, Lyapunov functions, nonlinear feedback, or optimal control formulations. Below we summarize foundational and recent high-quality publications, highlighting their key ideas and solutions:

## Energy-Based Swing-Up Controllers

**Åström & Furuta (2000, Automatica):** This seminal paper introduced an *energy control* strategy for pendulum swing-up. By neglecting the pendulum’s reaction on the drive arm (reducing the model to a single pendulum), they derived a simple *closed-form* law that pumps energy into the pendulum up to the level of the upright. In essence, the controller drives the pendulum’s total energy $E$ toward the desired value $E^*$ (energy at upright) by applying torque in phase with the pendulum motion. The proposed law (with $k>0$ and saturation at max torque $u_{\max}$) was:

$$u = \mathrm{sat}_{u_{\max}}\!\Big(k\,(E^* - E)\,\mathrm{sign}(\dot\theta\cos\theta)\Big)$$

which is bang-bang for large energy error and smooth near $E^*$. Using this law, the pendulum’s energy increases until it nearly reaches upright, where a balancing controller can take over.

**Fradkov’s Speed-Gradient Method:** *A. Fradkov* pioneered a general analytic method to induce oscillations in nonlinear systems by controlling their energy. In a 1996 paper, he applied the **speed-gradient (SG) algorithm** to the pendulum swing-up problem. The SG method yields an explicit feedback law that injects energy until the pendulum’s energy error is driven to zero. Fradkov’s controllers were proven to swing up the pendulum from rest, and he demonstrated convergence using Lyapunov arguments. These works provided a physics-based alternative to bang-bang control, ensuring the pendulum reaches the upright with finite energy. Notably, Fradkov *et al.* (1995) applied this to a *rotating (Furuta) pendulum*, and their method underpins many later energy controllers.

**Gordillo, Acosta & Aracil (2003, _Int. J. Control_):** This well-cited paper presented a *“new swing-up law”* for the **full 4th-order Furuta pendulum model**, overcoming the simplification in Åström–Furuta’s approach. They applied Fradkov’s SG technique together with a model reduction (Routh’s method) to derive an **analytical nonlinear feedback** that respects the coupled arm–pendulum dynamics. The resulting law smoothly drives the pendulum along a *homoclinic orbit* toward the upright, while keeping the base rotation bounded. A key benefit is that it requires arbitrarily small control effort (it gradually pumps energy). The authors report that, unlike some 4th-order swing-up laws, their control signal vanishes as the pendulum approaches the upright (energy target). This paper (and the team’s earlier conferences) is a cornerstone in *nonlinear swing-up control*, confirming that a smooth, global solution is possible for the rotary pendulum.

**Fantoni & Lozano (2002, _Int. J. Control_):** Fantoni and Lozano proposed an **energy-based Lyapunov approach** to swing up the Furuta pendulum around its natural *homoclinic trajectory*. They designed a feedback law (using system passivity properties) that raises the pendulum’s energy to the upright level and makes the upright equilibrium *almost* attractive. In their method, the pendulum repeatedly swings through increasing amplitudes until reaching the top, at which point a seamless transition to stabilization occurs. The controller design was guided by a Lyapunov function and ensured that the *upper equilibrium* is achieved with the pendulum’s total energy shaped appropriately. This work demonstrated a rigorous Lyapunov-based swing-up solution on the full rotary pendulum, emphasizing analytical stability proofs.

**Rodríguez-Cortés (2019, Kybernetika):** This paper introduces a new, smooth nonlinear swing-up controller for the Furuta pendulum, based on the Total Energy Control System (TECS) approach. Using a combination of energy control and feedback linearization, the law commands the total energy rate rather than just the energy level. This ensures that the pendulum’s energy grows appropriately while also keeping the rotation of the base arm bounded. Once the pendulum reaches the vicinity of the upright, a local stabilizer takes over. Real-time experimental results show effectiveness and practical feasibility. This approach generalizes previous energy-based designs (e.g., Åström–Furuta, Fradkov) for the complete Furuta model and is a notable modern addition to the literature.

**Passivity-Based Energy Shaping (2002–2007):** Another line of research used *nonlinear passivity and energy shaping* methods to derive exact swing-up controls. Notably, **Ortega, Spong *et al.* (2002, IEEE TAC)** applied the IDA-PBC (Interconnection and Damping Assignment – Passivity-Based Control) framework to the cart-pole and Furuta pendulum. They analytically designed a feedback that molds the system’s Lagrangian such that the inverted pendulum becomes a stable equilibrium of a new “target” energy function. This yields a **closed-form control law** (solution of “matching” equations) that globally stabilizes the pendulum at top. Similarly, Shiriaev *et al.* (2000) analyzed the global domain of attraction of energy-shaping controllers. These works show that by adding an appropriate nonlinear damping and shaping the potential energy, one can obtain an **exact swing-up + stabilize controller** from physical principles. (In practice, due to the pendulum’s topology, global smooth stabilization requires hybrid or almost-global designs, as discussed next.)

## Nonlinear Feedback and Lyapunov Methods

**Angeli (2001, Automatica):** D. Angeli presented a landmark result: an **almost-global stabilizing feedback** for the inverted pendulum on a cart, using a single continuous control law. By cleverly blending energy-shaping with state-dependent switching (in a smooth way), Angeli’s controller is *analytic* and renders the upright attractive for all initial conditions except a small set of measure zero (the downward equilibrium). This was one of the first proofs that a *non-hybrid* feedback could swing up and stabilize a pendulum “almost” globally. The design was Lyapunov-based and avoided any numerical tuning – a purely analytical construction. It inspired similar results for the rotary pendulum: e.g. **Aguilar-Ibáñez *et al.* (2006, CDC)** who crafted a Lyapunov function for the Furuta pendulum and derived a continuous control that swings it up to the top. These works leveraged the pendulum’s natural energy and damping properties to ensure convergence without external trajectory planning.

**Partial Feedback Linearization (Spong, 1994):** In the context of the *Acrobot* (a 2-link underactuated pendulum), M. W. Spong developed a partial feedback linearization strategy that has been influential for pendulum swing-up. By algebraically canceling the dynamics of one link, he obtained an equivalent single-pendulum system for which an energy-based swing-up law can be applied. This yields an explicit control that drives the unactuated link (the “pendulum” part) to upright while accounting for the coupling with the actuated link. The method, presented at SYROCO 1994, effectively creates *unstable zero dynamics* that propel the pendulum upward. Although developed for a different mechanism, this analytic approach is closely related to Furuta pendulum solutions – it underscores how model-based nonlinear inversion can simplify the swing-up problem to a tractable form.

**Virtual Holonomic Constraints (Shiriaev *et al.*, 2007):** An advanced nonlinear method is to enforce a *virtual holonomic constraint (VHC)* – a predefined functional relationship between the arm angle and pendulum angle – such that the system evolves on a lower-dimensional manifold. **Shiriaev *et al.*** applied this to the Furuta pendulum by choosing a *target oscillation* (periodic orbit) and designing a control that makes that orbit exponentially stable. In their IEEE Trans. Robotics 2007 paper, they derived an analytic feedback law that stabilizes a **family of periodic swing motions** (e.g. the pendulum swinging back-and-forth through the upright, or around). The constraint is chosen so that when the pendulum’s motion obeys it, energy gradually transfers from the arm to lift the pendulum. The result is a closed-form orbit controller: the pendulum+arm angle satisfy a fixed relation (like $\theta_2(f(\theta_1))$) and the pendulum oscillates until reaching the top. This orbit can then be damped out to balance. Later works (La Hera *et al.*, *Mechatronics* 2009) refined this approach with experimental validation. The VHC method is fully analytical (one solves for a constraint polynomial and associated input) and proved effective in achieving swing-up via *stable oscillations* rather than direct point stabilization.

## Optimal Control Solutions

Several researchers have tackled swing-up via **optimal control theory**, deriving explicit solutions from the calculus of variations. A classic result is that *minimum-time* swing-up of a pendulum is achieved by **bang-bang** torque inputs – i.e. full positive or negative torque, with at most one switch if torque is unbounded. In practice with bounded torque, the optimal solution involves multiple switches at precise states. **Åström & Furuta (2000)** already compared their energy-shaping design to the minimum-time strategy, noting the latter is faster but more sensitive. Subsequent works solved the two-point boundary value problem for the pendulum analytically. For example, **Chernousko & Reshmin (2007, _Nonlinear Dynamics_)** derived a **time-optimal feedback law** for the planar pendulum. Using Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle, they obtained the switching curves in the phase plane that partition when to apply maximum positive vs. negative torque. The outcome is an *exact state-feedback map* (constructed with some numerical assistance) that guarantees the fastest swing-up for a given torque bound. Similarly, **Horibe & Sakamoto (2018, IEEE TCST)** addressed a *combined swing-up and stabilization* optimal control problem. They employed a **stable manifold method**, effectively solving the Hamilton–Jacobi Bellman equation (HJB) for the rotary pendulum. The authors obtained a single nonlinear feedback law that drives the pendulum from the bottom to the top *optimally* (with respect to a cost) and keeps it there, by computing the stable manifold of the augmented system’s Hamiltonian dynamics. This represents a **closed-form optimal solution** in the sense that the HJB was solved (offline) to give an explicit control policy, rather than just an open-loop trajectory. While heavy in analysis, it demonstrates that exact optimal feedback can be found for underactuated swing-up.

## Summary

The Furuta pendulum and cart-pole have inspired a rich body of analytical control designs. Early energy-based controllers showed the effectiveness of simple physics-inspired laws. Later, nonlinear control theory contributed methods like feedback linearization, controlled Lagrangians, and passivity-based shaping to achieve global results. Recent optimal control and geometric methods push further, seeking single-law solutions that are both optimal and global. All these works eschew numerical “black-box” tuning in favor of explicit formulas derived from the system’s dynamics. Together they form a foundational literature for swing-up control, providing deep insights and **closed-form solutions** for this quintessential underactuated problem.
